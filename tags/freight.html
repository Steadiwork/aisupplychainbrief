Thieves have stolen more than $100 million in data center freight since June

More than $100 million. That's how much data center cargo thieves have taken off U.S. highways since June, NBC News reported on October 26, based on accounts from industry executives, consultants and investigators who advise police. Targets include servers, memory chips, fiber-optic cable, copper wire and data center power and cooling gear. In early June, a trailer with about $13 million in server equipment headed for Fayetteville, Georgia, was among the losses. The method is mostly digital: thieves take over freight broker accounts on load boards, post fake loads, send malware links to carriers that respond, then use those carriers' accounts to bid on real shipments. Some use fake driver's licenses, and in one large theft the tracking signal was spoofed. Scott Cornell, chair of TAPA Americas, told FreightWaves in August that two escorted loads were lost after escorts were deliberately hit, and he tied both to drivers who weren't properly vetted. Why it matters: the weak point is carrier and driver verification, which buyers and logistics teams can check before the load moves.

Motive's new AI agents call truck drivers and open work orders on their own

It calls the driver itself. Motive launched Agents on October 19: AI that watches the safety and operations data on its fleet platform and acts, calling drivers in the cab through its Atlas assistant when it spots fatigue or bad weather ahead, opening a maintenance work order and marking a truck out of service when a fault code meets the fleet's rules, and prompting drivers to cut idling. Fleets choose the triggers and actions. Agents are available now to Motive customers, plain-language goals are promised for early next year, and Motive hasn't published results from fleets using it.

An AI operator is auditing every LTL invoice at one freight broker

Nearly 3,500 audit decisions in two months, at 97% accuracy by the vendor's own count. That's what startup Evos reports for the AI operator that freight brokerage MyFreightWorld uses to check every LTL carrier invoice against its TMS; Evos says it closed out more than $600,000 of invoice value in the last five weeks. Evos came out of stealth on October 14 with $9 million in pre-seed funding, and it hasn't disclosed pricing or how much money the audits actually recovered.

Kodiak's autonomous trucks now run reefer and dry freight to Laredo

Kodiak AI and carrier Charger Logistics are hauling refrigerated and dry-van freight for food, beverage and consumer-goods shippers on the 435-mile lane between Dallas and Laredo, the busiest US land port of entry. The first load ran September 8, and a safety driver is still on board until Kodiak finishes its safety case. Charger owns the trucks and runs Kodiak's driving system under a driver-as-a-service model, and it has a separate autonomy deal with Aurora on the same lane.

A Texas carrier adds Waabi's autonomous trucks to haul repeat freight

Warrior Logistics, an asset-based carrier with 200+ trucks, is running recurring food, beverage and consumer-goods loads on Waabi Driver-powered trucks between Dallas and Houston. Waabi says those routes have so far delivered 100% on-time pickup and delivery with no safety incidents, though neither company shared truck or load counts. Waabi also says its load volume on the lane is up more than 80% this year.

Samsara opens its no-code AI agent builder to all fleet customers

Samsara made Agent Studio generally available on October 13. Fleets can build chat and voice agents on their own operating data, for example to compile reports, flag possible hours-of-service violations, or answer driver questions from company policy. Samsara says agent use is now running at about seven times its June launch pace, but it hasn't said how many customers run agents in production.
