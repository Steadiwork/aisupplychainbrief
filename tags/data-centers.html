AI data center tenants now ask $5 million a day when builds run late

$5 million a day, up from $1 million this spring. That's what tenants in new AI data center leases are asking developers to pay when power and buildings aren't ready on time, The Information reported on October 25. Insurance broker Marsh designed a delay-in-delivery policy this spring for the $1 million case, then saw new deals ask for five times that within months. Marsh says contractors and suppliers can be asked to backstop those penalties in their own agreements, and that guarantees now cover equipment lead times and energization dates.

So whatCheck that delay damages flowed down to you are capped and tied to delays you control.

NVIDIA's DSX POD ships AI data centers as factory-built containers

Six months down to as little as 14 days. That's NVIDIA's estimate for bringing AI computing online at a prepared site with DSX POD, a design it introduced on October 21 in which partners build complete data-center modules inside containers. Each holds seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks plus networking, cabling and liquid cooling, all tested before shipping, Data Center Knowledge reported. NVIDIA names 17 partners, including Dell, HPE, Lenovo, Schneider Electric and Vertiv, with deliveries from early 2027. NVIDIA's own FAQ is clear that sites still need power, cooling water and network hookups ready first.

Read the story →

So whatThe 14 days starts only when site power and cooling are ready. Buy those first; get delivery slots in writing.

Micron's Taiwan union plans a Thursday strike at an AI memory hub

A strike set for Thursday, October 22, at Micron's plant in Taoyuan, Taiwan, where the company makes DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI servers. The Taoyuan union announced it on October 19 to back a second union in a bonus dispute, timing it to that union's mediation meeting with Micron the same day, Reuters reported. The union hasn't said how long the walkout will last, and TrendForce says any hit to output depends on its size, its length and Micron's contingency plans.

So whatCheck which memory parts trace to Micron Taiwan and get lead-time status in writing before anyone panic-buys.

Microsoft walks away from the UK's biggest planned AI data center over power

No grid power until the 2030s: that's what Nscale was told about its planned data center in Loughton, Essex, which was meant to house about 23,000 Nvidia chips and open next year. Microsoft, its only publicly announced customer for the site, has ended its agreement, the BBC and the Guardian reported on October 18; Microsoft declined to comment. Nscale says it remains committed, is exploring alternative power sources, and that its Microsoft projects in Norway, Portugal and the US are unaffected.

Read the story →

So whatA signed AI capacity deal is only as firm as the site's power connection, so get the power date in writing.

AI's appetite for memory just caused the worst quarter on record for smartphones

IDC's preliminary numbers show global smartphone shipments fell 22% year over year in the third quarter to about 255 million units, the steepest quarterly drop it has ever recorded. IDC blames the AI-driven memory shortage: memory costs are up roughly 350% from a year ago, and vendors have cut low-end models and raised prices. IDC expects pressure to last through 2027 and warns that some makers built ahead of next year's expected component squeeze, which could leave excess inventory if demand stays soft.

Read the story →

So whatIf any of your products use DRAM or flash, treat memory as a constrained, fast-moving cost input in your plan.

Adani and Jabil sign up to build AI racks and servers in India

Adani Enterprises and contract manufacturer Jabil signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding on October 13 for a 50:50 joint venture to make and assemble AI racks, servers, storage, networking gear, power distribution, liquid cooling and transformers in India, plus installation and commissioning. The companies are aiming for multi-gigawatt rack capacity for Indian and export customers. No investment figure or production date was given, and the deal still needs definitive agreements and approvals.

TSMC: AI chip demand is still bigger than its capacity, and it's scouting Texas

TSMC reported third-quarter revenue of about $46.7B, up roughly 51% from a year ago, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to $52.3B to $54.3B. Management said AI-related demand remains very strong and the gap between demand and supply is still large, with 2026 capital spending planned at $60B to $64B. The WSJ reported that TSMC has sent teams to Texas to evaluate expansion beyond Arizona.

Chip tariffs: the data-center exemption is still in limbo

January's Section 232 tariff put 25% on certain advanced logic chips but exempted chips headed for US data centers, pending a Commerce Department review that hasn't been published. On October 2, five tech trade groups including CCIA and TechNet asked the White House to keep any further chip, robotics and machinery tariffs targeted, and to extend any tariff offset program to downstream buyers. No second-phase decision has been announced.

GPU rental prices keep rising even as AI token prices fall

Posts on X highlighted that rental prices for top-end Nvidia GPUs climbed after per-token AI prices peaked, a classic Jevons effect: cheaper AI drives more usage. Silicon Data's B300 rental index launched near $6.95/GPU-hour, up roughly 44% since April. A separate marketplace tracker showed another weekly gain in early October.
