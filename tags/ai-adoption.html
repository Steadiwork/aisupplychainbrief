Amazon Business lets buyers ask spend questions in plain language

Two years of purchase history and more than 60 reporting fields now sit behind a typed question, according to the National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors' MDM. Amazon Business said on October 27 at its Reshape event in Nashville that its Business Assistant can now answer spend questions, track orders and approvals, and suggest how to allocate payments for U.S. customers, with existing permissions and approval rules left in place. It also launched Value Intelligence, a report that combines savings, efficiency and policy compliance, with methods customers can review and adjust.

Oracle launches AI agents that draft allocation plans and hunt maverick spend

Twenty-five new agentic applications became available across Oracle's Fusion cloud apps on October 26, and three are aimed at supply chain teams. Oracle says Supply Planning Allocation compares scenarios for splitting scarce materials and capacity across competing demand, Shipment Load Planning looks for load consolidation to cut transport cost, and Procurement Spend flags maverick spend and contract leakage. According to Oracle, each one prepares plans or recommendations that people review and approve.

83% of supply chain leaders say their people aren't ready to use AI

28% versus 71%. That's the AI project success rate for organizations whose people aren't ready, compared with those whose people are, in The People Paradox in Supply Chain AI Integration, a report the Association for Supply Chain Management (ASCM) and the Digital Supply Chain Institute (DSCI) released October 14. FreightWaves interviewed ASCM CEO Abe Eshkenazi about it on October 23. Of 107 qualified respondents, 83% said their employees are unprepared to use AI well, and 64% of the unprepared group is still stuck in pilots or planning. On a scale from 1 (no AI) to 5 (fully autonomous), adoption across 13 supply chain functions averaged 1.94. Demand planning led at 2.28; reverse logistics was last at 1.59. The top barriers tied at 33% each: missing skills and an unclear business case. Why it matters: involvement drops off below the top. 82% of C-suite respondents lead or scale AI work, but 70% of staff say they're aware of it without being involved. The people who run the process aren't helping shape the tool.

Siemens puts an AI agent to work on production planning at its Erlangen plant

Up to 20% lower costs from an AI agent that drafts production plans: that's Siemens' own estimate from its electronics factory in Erlangen, Germany, where it showed new tools built with NVIDIA on October 19. When a new order arrives, the agent generates several production scenarios, shows planners how each one scores on defined KPIs, and checks the proposals against a digital twin of the whole plant before anything changes on the floor. A second tool trains the plant's inspection cameras on AI-generated images of new circuit boards, so quality checks can be set up before the first prototype exists; Siemens says that cuts engineering effort by up to 85% and time to market by up to 40%. German tech site Dr. Web notes these are best-case figures from Siemens and haven't been independently checked. Why it matters: a planning agent can only rank options against goals someone has written down, such as on-time delivery, changeovers, overtime and material shortages. Plants that haven't agreed on those will get fast answers to the wrong question.

Free AI forecasting models just beat a classic method on grocery data

4.5% to 6.3% less forecast error, with no training on the data. That's what Superlinear found when it tested Mistral's new Timestral models and Google's TimesFM against ETS, a classic statistical method many planning systems still rely on, using Walmart M5 food sales and a fresh-food dataset. Mistral released Timestral on October 15: four open-weight time-series models, from 5 million to 209 million parameters, that produce probabilistic forecasts, accept known future inputs such as planned promotions, and run on an ordinary CPU. In Superlinear's test, the small 28M version came within 0.4% of the largest model's accuracy in about a fifth of the time. Two cautions: Superlinear says Timestral is free only for companies under $20 million in monthly revenue, and it advises judging a forecast on service level, waste and stock, not error alone.

Physical Intelligence opens its robot models to outside developers

Physical Intelligence launched a waitlisted beta of its Pi API on October 13. Teams upload their own robot data, fine-tune the company's general-purpose models on its managed infrastructure, and run them through a hosted service.
