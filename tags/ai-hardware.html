Taiwan's exports hit a record $96.76 billion on AI demand as Asia air space tightens

US$96.76 billion, up 58.9% from a year earlier. Taiwan's Ministry of Finance said on Monday that October exports set a monthly record, and it credited heavy investment in AI infrastructure. Shipments to the U.S. rose 55.9% to a record US$32.93 billion, CNA's Focus Taiwan reported, and the ministry expects US$94.2 billion to US$96.7 billion in November. C.H. Robinson's November air update says servers, chip equipment and data-center components from Taiwan and nearby origins are tightening trans-Pacific capacity, with Asia-origin rates expected to rise through November.

Eaton data-center orders more than double as electrical demand outruns new capacity

More than 100% growth in rolling 12-month data-center orders. That's what Eaton reported on its November 3 earnings call, alongside data-center revenue up about 65% and a ninth straight quarter of data-center growth above 35%. CEO Paulo Ruiz said Electrical Americas backlog is up 33% and "demand continues to run ahead of what we can convert," even as organic sales in that segment rose 22%. Electrical book-to-bill sat at 1.2, with Americas at 1.3, and total electrical backlog above $20 billion, up 46% year over year. The company also announced general price increases for Electrical Americas and Global effective January 2, 2027. Why it matters: AI data-center buildouts are pulling the same switchgear, UPS and distribution gear plants and warehouses buy for everyday work. Materials managers with electrical lines on open POs or 2027 capital plans may be working from lead times and quotes that no longer hold.

So whatRe-check electrical-gear lead times and quote validity this week; escalate slips over two weeks.

Emirates SkyCargo launches a dedicated air freight product for AI servers and chips

More than 700 tonnes in one month. That's how much AI infrastructure Emirates SkyCargo says it flew in July, and on November 3 it launched AI Tech, a product for server racks, semiconductors, GPUs and capital equipment. It adds priority loading, handling procedures that keep equipment in the right orientation, and airport-to-airport milestone tracking from its Dubai command centre; highly specialised machinery gets a station check first. The airline says it will run 23 freighters by March 2027, with main-deck room for racks that can weigh more than 3.5 tonnes.

Amazon can borrow up to $80 billion against the hardware it buys

Up to $80 billion, secured by the equipment itself. In its October 30 quarterly filing, Amazon disclosed a supplier-financing loan facility for "technology infrastructure" purchases that it can draw on from early 2027 through the third quarter of 2028, with each borrowing maturing five years later. Lenders get a security interest in certain purchased assets, unlike Amazon's bonds and credit lines, and the amount can vary with the timing of purchase orders and pricing terms. Bloomberg ties the move to Amazon's AI buildout; the filing names no lenders, suppliers or assets.

Thieves have stolen more than $100 million in data center freight since June

More than $100 million. That's how much data center cargo thieves have taken off U.S. highways since June, NBC News reported on October 26, based on accounts from industry executives, consultants and investigators who advise police. Targets include servers, memory chips, fiber-optic cable, copper wire and data center power and cooling gear. In early June, a trailer with about $13 million in server equipment headed for Fayetteville, Georgia, was among the losses. The method is mostly digital: thieves take over freight broker accounts on load boards, post fake loads, send malware links to carriers that respond, then use those carriers' accounts to bid on real shipments. Some use fake driver's licenses, and in one large theft the tracking signal was spoofed. Scott Cornell, chair of TAPA Americas, told FreightWaves in August that two escorted loads were lost after escorts were deliberately hit, and he tied both to drivers who weren't properly vetted. Why it matters: the weak point is carrier and driver verification, which buyers and logistics teams can check before the load moves.

So whatConfirm how carriers and drivers are verified before high-value AI hardware leaves the dock.

NVIDIA's DSX POD ships AI data centers as factory-built containers

Six months down to as little as 14 days. That's NVIDIA's estimate for bringing AI computing online at a prepared site with DSX POD, a design it introduced on October 21 in which partners build complete data-center modules inside containers. Each holds seven Vera Rubin NVL72 racks plus networking, cabling and liquid cooling, all tested before shipping, Data Center Knowledge reported. NVIDIA names 17 partners, including Dell, HPE, Lenovo, Schneider Electric and Vertiv, with deliveries from early 2027. NVIDIA's own FAQ is clear that sites still need power, cooling water and network hookups ready first.

So whatThe 14 days starts only when site power and cooling are ready. Buy those first; get delivery slots in writing.

Adani and Jabil sign up to build AI racks and servers in India

Adani Enterprises and contract manufacturer Jabil signed a non-binding memorandum of understanding on October 13 for a 50:50 joint venture to make and assemble AI racks, servers, storage, networking gear, power distribution, liquid cooling and transformers in India, plus installation and commissioning.
