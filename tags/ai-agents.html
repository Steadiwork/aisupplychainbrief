Walmart lets customers' AI agents shop for them, with spending limits and rules

$50, $100 or $200: those are the example spending limits Walmart gave on October 21 for AI agents that will shop on a customer's behalf. Walmart announced three pieces. Agent Profiles, launching within the next month, give each authorized agent its own profile inside a customer's account, so its purchases show up separately; customers either approve every order or set a limit. The Walmart Agent Gateway, an invitation-only beta for agent builders, lets AI assistants connect directly to Walmart and Sam's Club systems to search and buy, Reuters reported. And new terms say agents need approval by default, never see payment card details, and get blocked if they break the rules. The Verge notes the contrast with Amazon, which has blocked outside shopping agents. Why it matters: an agent buying "the usual" picks from what product listings and stock data tell it, and launch lands right before holiday peak. Wrong pack sizes or stale availability can now cost an order without a human ever seeing the shelf.

So whatAgents buy what the item data says. Check pack sizes and availability on your top online listings before peak.

Opply raises $7.5M for AI agents that place and pay purchase orders

15 times the gross revenue in 12 months. That's the growth Opply reported on October 21 as it raised $7.5 million in a round led by Chalfen Ventures. The London company's agents find suppliers, place and track purchase orders, check compliance documents and pay suppliers for food and drink brands, which repay Opply within 90 days, Tech Funding News reported. Opply cites customer cost cuts of 40%; Tech Funding News points out that part of that benefit comes from longer payment terms Opply extends, not only from better prices.

Siemens puts an AI agent to work on production planning at its Erlangen plant

Up to 20% lower costs from an AI agent that drafts production plans: that's Siemens' own estimate from its electronics factory in Erlangen, Germany, where it showed new tools built with NVIDIA on October 19. When a new order arrives, the agent generates several production scenarios, shows planners how each one scores on defined KPIs, and checks the proposals against a digital twin of the whole plant before anything changes on the floor. A second tool trains the plant's inspection cameras on AI-generated images of new circuit boards, so quality checks can be set up before the first prototype exists; Siemens says that cuts engineering effort by up to 85% and time to market by up to 40%. German tech site Dr. Web notes these are best-case figures from Siemens and haven't been independently checked. Why it matters: a planning agent can only rank options against goals someone has written down, such as on-time delivery, changeovers, overtime and material shortages. Plants that haven't agreed on those will get fast answers to the wrong question.

So whatA planning agent is only as good as the KPIs it ranks plans by. Write yours down before any pilot.

Motive's new AI agents call truck drivers and open work orders on their own

It calls the driver itself. Motive launched Agents on October 19: AI that watches the safety and operations data on its fleet platform and acts, calling drivers in the cab through its Atlas assistant when it spots fatigue or bad weather ahead, opening a maintenance work order and marking a truck out of service when a fault code meets the fleet's rules, and prompting drivers to cut idling. Fleets choose the triggers and actions. Agents are available now to Motive customers, plain-language goals are promised for early next year, and Motive hasn't published results from fleets using it.

BMW's procurement agent answers capacity questions in minutes, and its language model does no math

Weeks down to a few minutes: that's how long BMW says it now takes to answer a question like "can we build more iX3s?" with its procurement agent, Mr. Flexibility, which it showed to journalists, automotiveIT reported on October 12. The agent pulls together the production program, agreed supplier capacity and engineering data, including battery-cell capacity at 13 supplier sites. The design is the lesson: the language model "calculates nothing," product owner Maik Leuthold told automotiveIT. Tested, rule-based tools do the math, each user sees only what they're allowed to see, stale data gets flagged, and people make the call. The weeks-to-minutes figure is BMW's own.

Read the story →

So whatLet AI pick the tool but keep the math in calculations you trust, and date-stamp your supplier capacity data.

An AI operator is auditing every LTL invoice at one freight broker

Nearly 3,500 audit decisions in two months, at 97% accuracy by the vendor's own count. That's what startup Evos reports for the AI operator that freight brokerage MyFreightWorld uses to check every LTL carrier invoice against its TMS; Evos says it closed out more than $600,000 of invoice value in the last five weeks. Kao's AI agent drafts demand plans, and people still make the call

In an English release this week, Japanese consumer-goods maker Kao detailed Kao-MIKOMIL, an AI agent that layers planned promotions, channels and pricing on top of forecasts from its in-house AI forecasting model, then proposes a demand plan with its reasoning.
