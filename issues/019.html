Forty weeks for a capacitor

1. Multilayer ceramic capacitors now quote 40 weeks as AI servers soak up supply

Forty weeks. That's the lead time TrendForce now lists for multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs), against about 12 weeks in a balanced market, in its November 16 weekly radar, which moved the part to "Very Tight" as AI-driven demand presses on supply. It was 32 weeks at the end of September, Korea's ZDNet reported, citing TrendForce, and TrendForce had it at 38 weeks on November 2. ZDNet added that an AI server rack can carry up to 600,000 MLCCs, more than ten times a general server. TrendForce's November 13 bulletin says the shortage has spread from servers to networking and optical communications parts, new supply capacity is mostly delayed beyond 2028, long-term contracts are tightening access to high-end parts, and the gap is likely to last into the first half of 2027. Its October 29 release said talks on first-quarter 2027 prices were set to start in November. Why it matters: the squeeze has already moved beyond AI servers, and a capacitor you never think about can hold a finished board.

So what for materials managersFind the MLCCs on your bills of material where confirmed coverage ends before the quoted lead time, and start alternate-part reviews on those first.

What to do next
  1. Pull every MLCC on your active bills of material and list manufacturer part number, case size, capacitance, voltage rating, weekly usage, stock on hand, confirmed receipts and quoted lead time.
  2. Ask your distributors and contract manufacturers in writing which of those part numbers are on allocation, whether quoted lead times or prices changed since September, and whether first-quarter 2027 price notices are coming.
  3. Where confirmed coverage ends before the quoted lead time, start an alternate-part review with engineering, matching voltage rating, dielectric, temperature range and tolerance, not just case size and capacitance.
You are a materials manager sizing supply gaps on constrained capacitors. Inputs, anonymized (codes instead of supplier and part names), one row per part: part code, case size, capacitance, voltage rating, supplier code, number of approved sources, average weekly usage, on-hand quantity, confirmed receipts (quantity and due week), quoted lead time (weeks), allocation status (yes/no/unknown), and the earliest week an approved alternate could be in production (or "none").
[PASTE TABLE]
1) For each part, calculate weeks of coverage from on-hand stock plus confirmed receipts, and the week stock runs out if a receipt slips by [4] weeks. Show your math for the first row.
2) Calculate the gap quantity: usage from today until the earlier of the quoted lead time or the alternate-ready week, minus on-hand and confirmed receipts.
3) Flag parts with a positive gap, a single approved source, allocation marked yes or unknown, or no alternate.
4) Rank flagged parts by gap quantity times weeks short.
5) For the top 10, suggest one next step each (confirm allocation in writing, request a buffer order for approval, start an alternate review, or watch) with a one-line reason.
Output: a table with columns part, supplier, sources, coverage (weeks), lead time (weeks), alternate-ready week, gap quantity, flags, next step. Keep it under 300 words. Use only the data I give. List any missing or inconsistent data instead of guessing.

Tip of the DayFor Material managers

Have AI find the open orders you won't need when lead times ease

Long lead times tempt everyone to order early, order twice, or ask two distributors for the same need; as one post on X put it this week, blown-out lead times breed phantom double orders. Export open PO lines with on-hand stock and usage by part, swap supplier and part names for codes, and have your approved AI tool flag orders that push coverage past your target and check whether each is still inside its change window. You get a short list to reschedule or cancel while suppliers can still say yes, instead of a dock full of last quarter's nerves.

You are a materials manager reviewing open purchase orders for excess on order. Inputs, anonymized (codes instead of supplier and part names). Table A, one row per part: part code, on-hand quantity, average weekly usage, target coverage (weeks), quoted lead time (weeks). Table B, open PO lines: PO number, line, part code, supplier code, open quantity, due date, last date the supplier accepts a reschedule or cancellation.

3) For flagged parts, list the PO lines that, removed or pushed out (latest due date first), bring coverage back to target. Also flag the same part ordered from more than one supplier code with due dates within [2] weeks of each other.
4) Mark each listed line "still changeable" or "past window" using the change date.
5) Draft one short, neutral reschedule or cancellation request per supplier code for its changeable lines.

LogisticsAI in supply chain

2. Veson hands chartering, voyage updates and claims prep to 11 AI workflows

Eleven prebuilt workflows. That's what Veson Nautical released on November 18 for chartering, voyage operations and claims teams that use its IMOS freight software. They triage incoming cargo opportunities, process itinerary updates for review before records change, and match statements of facts to claims while flagging differences between counterparty invoices and existing calculations, all inside each user's existing permissions.

So what for logistics leadsBefore an AI workflow touches voyage records or demurrage claims, decide which updates it may draft and which a person must approve, and keep a log of both.

WarehousingAI in supply chain

3. A self-learning picking arm reaches 350 units an hour in a 3PL pilot

Up to 350 units an hour. That's what Carrousel AI, a robotic picking arm working at an AutoStore grid, picked soon after going live at Bleckmann's Bergen op Zoom site, the fashion and lifestyle logistics provider said on November 17. The three-year pilot with AutoStore and Berkshire Grey uses machine learning to choose a suction cup for each item and adapt to new SKUs, and Bleckmann's Dutch release says the rate could reach 650 an hour once fully tuned.

So what for warehouse managersWhen a robot vendor quotes a start-up rate and a "fully tuned" rate, write the tuning period and the target rate on your own SKU mix into the pilot terms.

ProcurementAI in supply chain

4. Infor connects Claude to its manufacturing ERP, with approval before every change

A replacement purchase order, placed from a chat. That's one task Infor says buyers can do with Infor Industry AI for Claude, announced with Anthropic on November 12: see which production or customer orders a late receipt will affect, identify alternative suppliers and order. Infor says each change needs the user's explicit approval, existing Infor permissions still decide who can do what, and actions are recorded in Infor; it's available to select Infor Velocity Suite customers who bring their own Claude license.

So what for buyersBefore connecting a chat assistant to your ERP, check that its purchase-order rights follow each buyer's own approval limits, not a shared admin account.
