A million robots, measured in cost to serve

Inside today: Amazon passes one million robots · Kodiak signs 75 driverless trucks · Nvidia weighs backing a data center loan · Berkshire Grey teaches robots to unload trailers

Amazon says more than one million robots now work in its fulfillment network, and it doubled its fleet of robotic arms this year. The number your leadership will borrow is not the robot count, though. It's cost to serve, and that's the yardstick every automation pitch now has to beat.

Four stories from the last three days, across warehousing, freight and data-center financing. Every item links to the original source; summaries are our own.

Top storyWarehousingAI in supply chain

1. Amazon says it now runs more than a million warehouse robots, and the last 100 yards are next

More than one million. That's how many robots Amazon now has in its fulfillment network, CEO Andy Jassy said on the company's third-quarter call on October 29. Amazon doubled its fleet of robotic arms such as Cardinal and Sparrow in 2026, said new same-day facilities will rely heavily on robotics, and is exploring robots for the last 100 yards to the customer's door. Jassy said deployment depends on whether the machines improve productivity, safety, cost and delivery speed. Amazon also said it lowered its overall cost to serve in the quarter through better inventory placement, shorter shipping distances and package consolidation: shipping costs rose 11% against 12% unit growth, or 8% excluding fuel and line-haul inflation. On the same call it kept 2026 cash capital spending at about $220 billion, mostly for AWS, with meaningfully more planned for 2027. Why it matters: the largest shipper around is scoring automation on cost to serve, not robot count, and your next automation business case will be judged the same way.

So what for supply chain managersKnow your own cost to serve per order, split by labor, shipping and line haul, before anyone pitches you robots or asks why Amazon's costs grow slower than volume.

What to do next
  1. Pull cost to serve per order (or per unit) for the last four quarters and split it into fulfillment labor, outbound shipping and line haul, so you can see which part grows faster than volume.
  2. List your three most repetitive, high-volume warehouse tasks with today's cost, error rate and injury record for each, so automation proposals are compared against real numbers.
  3. For any automation request in the 2027 budget, add one line showing the expected change in cost to serve, not just labor hours saved.
You are a supply chain cost analyst breaking down cost to serve. Inputs, anonymized, one row per quarter: quarter, orders shipped, units shipped, fulfillment labor cost, outbound shipping cost, line-haul cost, fuel surcharge, other fulfillment cost.
[PASTE TABLE]
1) Calculate cost per order and cost per unit for each quarter, in total and for each cost line.
2) Compare each cost line's growth with order and unit growth, quarter over quarter and year over year.
3) Show shipping cost growth with and without fuel surcharge.
4) Name the two cost lines growing fastest relative to volume and one likely driver to check for each.
Output: a table with columns quarter, cost per order, labor per order, shipping per order, line haul per order, growth vs volume; then the two lines to investigate. Keep it under 300 words. Use anonymized data only. List any missing or inconsistent data instead of guessing.

Tip of the DayFor Supply chain managers

Have AI build the award scorecard before the quotes arrive

Once prices are in, everyone suddenly remembers that price was always the top priority. Agree the scoring weights with quality, engineering and finance before bids open, then ask your approved AI tool to turn those priorities into a weighted scorecard with plain scoring rules. Date it and share it before anyone opens a quote.

You are a sourcing lead setting award criteria before an RFQ closes. Inputs, anonymized: category description, team priorities with a rough importance for each (for example price, quality history, lead time, capacity, payment terms, risk), any must-have requirements, number of bidders expected.
[PASTE PRIORITIES]
1) Turn the priorities into 4 to 6 scoring criteria with weights that add up to 100.
2) For each criterion, write a 1 to 5 scoring rule with a concrete description of a 1, a 3 and a 5.
3) List must-haves as pass or fail gates, scored before the weighted criteria.
4) Flag any criteria that overlap or that we can't measure from a typical quote.
Output: a table with columns criterion, weight, how to score 1/3/5, data source; then the pass or fail gates and the flags. Keep it under 300 words. Use anonymized data only. List any missing or inconsistent data instead of guessing.

2. Kodiak signs a carrier to run 75 of its driverless trucks on Texas and Sun Belt lanes

Seventy-five trucks, owned by the carrier, driven by software. On October 28 Kodiak AI signed a binding agreement with DTL Transport, a Fresno, California carrier that runs team loads for grocers, to put the Kodiak Driver on 75 DTL-owned trucks for a per-mile fee. The first lanes are Interstate 10 between Dallas and El Paso and Interstate 20 between Dallas and Atlanta. FreightWaves notes Kodiak has not yet run a driverless truck on a public highway, and the per-mile rate, contract term and start date were not disclosed.

So what for logistics leadsIf you ship on Texas or I-20 lanes, ask your carriers whether autonomous capacity is in their 2027 plans and how they would price it.

3. Nvidia is in talks to guarantee debt for a data center Anthropic plans to use

Billions in guarantees, not yet signed. Nvidia is in talks to give AI cloud provider Nscale billions of dollars in credit guarantees to back debt for a West Virginia data center that Anthropic plans to use, The Information reported on October 29, according to Reuters. The support would help fund power infrastructure and construction, and terms could still change. Nscale's September prospectus says Anthropic can end a delivery tranche without liability if Nscale misses its delivery window.

So what for procurement leadersWhen a capacity or data-center supplier's delivery dates depend on financing that hasn't closed, ask for funding status and milestone dates before you plan around their schedule.

WarehousingAI in supply chain

4. Berkshire Grey adds AI perception to its trailer-unloading robot

Trailer unloading is the dock job robots keep coming back to. On October 29 Berkshire Grey, a SoftBank Group subsidiary, said its BG Pilot software now gives its Scoop unloader real-time visual and 3D perception to find trailer walls, spot obstructions and flag items that need a person. The update also adds AI orchestration for store replenishment and machine-learning box selection for packing. The company says new business opportunities rose more than 65% over the past six months.

So what for warehouse managersBefore a trailer-unloading demo, send vendors photos of your worst mixed loads and ask what share of items their system hands back to a person.
