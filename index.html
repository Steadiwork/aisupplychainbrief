SpaceX moves turbine blade casting in-house to get around a 12-to-18-month wait

Everything but the blades and vanes can ship 12 to 18 months sooner. That's what turbine suppliers tell SpaceX, Elon Musk said on the company's October 20 earnings call. His answer, confirmed on X on August 29, is a SpaceX foundry in Bastrop, Texas, that will cast those parts in-house for the gas turbines that power its AI data centers. SpaceX job postings describe a foundry still being built and staffed, and no production date is public. Analysts cited by Quartz say a new plant like this takes at least four years, and its vacuum furnaces take more than two years to procure. Meanwhile Howmet, which Quartz says holds roughly half the global market for these turbine parts, reported gas turbine revenue up 45% in the third quarter. TechCrunch noted that gas turbines already running at data centers face federal lawsuits and health studies over emissions. Why it matters: one part sets the whole lead time, and SpaceX's response is to try to own that part rather than order the rest sooner.

Infor lets planners and buyers approve ERP changes from inside Claude

Fix the purchase order without leaving the chat. Infor and Anthropic said on November 12 that select Infor customers can connect Claude to Infor's manufacturing systems, ask why a production order is at risk, and approve actions such as creating a purchase order or reallocating stock. Infor says every change needs the user's explicit approval, and existing Infor permissions decide who can see and do what.
