A robot-run U.S. factory says it builds circuit boards in 4 days, at China prices

Four days, not nine. That's the average build-and-ship time CircuitHub says its automated circuit board factory in South Deerfield, Massachusetts, now hits, compared with a typical quick-turn shop in China. The company announced on October 26 that the factory, called the Grid, has reached price parity with similar Chinese shops and is expanding capacity fivefold, with a UK site planned for 2027. Levitating movers carry each board between robots that print solder paste, place parts, solder and inspect, and AI routes every board on its own path, so there are no changeovers between designs. CircuitHub says one unit each of 100 designs runs as easily as 100 units of one design, and that it has shipped more than 2.2 million boards. Why it matters: CircuitHub says 95% of electronics projects involve fewer than 10,000 units, and those small, high-mix orders are where offshore lead time and freight hurt most. Price parity is the company's own claim, so test it with a quote.

Oracle launches AI agents that draft allocation plans and hunt maverick spend

Twenty-five new agentic applications became available across Oracle's Fusion cloud apps on October 26, and three are aimed at supply chain teams. Oracle says Supply Planning Allocation compares scenarios for splitting scarce materials and capacity across competing demand, Shipment Load Planning looks for load consolidation to cut transport cost, and Procurement Spend flags maverick spend and contract leakage. According to Oracle, each one prepares plans or recommendations that people review and approve.

TSMC weighs up to 20% higher prices for AI chip packaging

Six to eight months. That's how long equipment lead times for advanced chip packaging have stretched, up from three to six, as TSMC weighs raising prices for its CoWoS packaging by 10% to 20%, Taiwan's Commercial Times reported, citing supply chain sources. CoWoS joins AI processors to high-bandwidth memory in data center accelerators, and demand has spread from GPUs to server CPUs and custom chips. TSMC has not confirmed the plan. TrendForce, citing the Liberty Times, says TSMC has also set wafer price increases of 3% to 6% starting January 2027.
