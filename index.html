2026-11-02 · Three trade clocks run out the same week

TradeAI in supply chain

178 tariff exclusions, U.S. port fees and China's export-control pause all hit their end dates the week of November 9

One week, three expiry dates. All 178 remaining Section 301 product exclusions on Chinese goods end at 11:59 p.m. ET on November 9 under the extension USTR published in December 2025, and the International Compliance Professionals Association's tracker said on November 1 that no notice extending them had been published. Goods entered on or after November 10 that relied on an exclusion would pay the full List rate, which runs from 7.5% to 25%. The suspension of U.S. port fees on Chinese-operated and Chinese-built vessels ends the same night: USTR proposed on October 26 to extend it to January 9, 2027, but had not finalized that as of November 1. On China's side, the legal text suspending its October 2025 export controls on rare earths, battery materials and superhard materials still reads "until November 10, 2026," even though Beijing said the wider trade arrangement now runs to January 10, 2027, according to a review of the texts by Nami Tech Solutions. Why it matters: buyers who rely on an exclusion, ship on Chinese-built vessels or source those materials could see cost or licensing changes within days.

Amazon can borrow up to $80 billion against the hardware it buys

Up to $80 billion, secured by the equipment itself. In its October 30 quarterly filing, Amazon disclosed a supplier-financing loan facility for "technology infrastructure" purchases that it can draw on from early 2027 through the third quarter of 2028, with each borrowing maturing five years later. Lenders get a security interest in certain purchased assets, unlike Amazon's bonds and credit lines, and the amount can vary with the timing of purchase orders and pricing terms. Bloomberg ties the move to Amazon's AI buildout; the filing names no lenders, suppliers or assets.

AI agents cut Worldwide Express's freight billing exception backlog by 75%

From $15 million to $4 million. That's how far the backlog of unresolved LTL freight billing exceptions fell at Worldwide Express in the six months after it launched three AI agents built on Amazon Bedrock, according to an AWS case study published October 30. The agents handle invoice mismatches, pricing variances and accessorial charges such as liftgate fees, each checking that carrier's own billing rules, and people approved every recommendation until accuracy passed 99%. AWS says the agents now close about 10,000 exceptions a week with an error rate under 1%.
