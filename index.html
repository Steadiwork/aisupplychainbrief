2026-10-28 · Thieves are hijacking data center freight

LogisticsSupply chain for AI

Thieves have stolen more than $100 million in data center freight since June

More than $100 million. That's how much data center cargo thieves have taken off U.S. highways since June, NBC News reported on October 26, based on accounts from industry executives, consultants and investigators who advise police. Targets include servers, memory chips, fiber-optic cable, copper wire and data center power and cooling gear. In early June, a trailer with about $13 million in server equipment headed for Fayetteville, Georgia, was among the losses. The method is mostly digital: thieves take over freight broker accounts on load boards, post fake loads, send malware links to carriers that respond, then use those carriers' accounts to bid on real shipments. Some use fake driver's licenses, and in one large theft the tracking signal was spoofed. Scott Cornell, chair of TAPA Americas, told FreightWaves in August that two escorted loads were lost after escorts were deliberately hit, and he tied both to drivers who weren't properly vetted. Why it matters: the weak point is carrier and driver verification, which buyers and logistics teams can check before the load moves.

SK hynix says its DRAM and HBM output is effectively sold out through 2029

Sold out through 2029. SK hynix said on its third-quarter earnings call on October 28 that, counting long-term agreements and deals under discussion, its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory output for the next three years is effectively spoken for, and it expects memory demand to outrun supply through 2030. The company said customer requests for HBM are more than twice what it can supply, and that long-term agreements already cover more than half its capacity for 2027 and 2028. It also expects PC and phone shipments to fall as memory costs rise.

SAP starts sending warehouse tasks straight to robots, and logging what they did

A humanoid robot at Arçelik-LG now takes its inbound storage tasks from SAP and records the result there. SAP showed the setup at TechEd on October 27 as part of new physical AI capabilities that send tasks from SAP Extended Warehouse Management to robots and write status and exceptions back. In a proof of concept at Intel's Chengdu site, SAP and Intel say material moves were automated end to end, with shorter transport distances and less manual handling; neither company gave figures.
