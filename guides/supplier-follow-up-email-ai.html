Email to a supplier for a delayed delivery: a 4-step escalation ladder (with samples and an AI prompt)

Short answerName the PO, part and quantity, the date it was due, and what you need: a confirmed ship date, the reason, and a recovery plan, by a specific reply-by time. Keep the first message friendly. If there's no answer, get firmer in the next one, then escalate to the supplier's manager with your need date.

Fictional exampleExample Fasteners Co., PO 4471, part EX-220, Sam, Jordan and A. Buyer are made up. Don't paste real pricing, customer names or export-controlled data into an AI tool; see the data-safety checklist.

When a purchase order is late, most buyers need wording that is firm without burning the relationship. The one-shot late-PO prompt in ChatGPT prompts for procurement (#p11) drafts a single email. This page adds the ladder: which message to send when, how the tone and the asks change, and who to escalate to.

Worked example: PO 4471, from check-in to escalation

PO 4471: 2,000 steel brackets (part EX-220). Confirmed ship date Oct 1; the buyer's line needs them on Oct 10. Supplier rep "Sam", operations manager "Jordan", buyer "A. Buyer".

Step 0 — pre-ship check (Sept 26)

Hi Sam, quick check on PO 4471 (2,000 brackets, part EX-220), confirmed to ship Oct 1. Are you still on track? If anything has changed, please send the new ship date and quantity by end of day Sept 29, so I can update our plan. Thanks, A. Buyer

Step 1 — missed date (Oct 2)

Hi Sam, PO 4471 (2,000 brackets) was due to ship Oct 1 and I don't see an ASN or tracking yet. Can you send by 3 p.m. tomorrow, Oct 3: (1) the confirmed ship date, (2) the reason for the delay, and (3) whether a partial shipment can go sooner? Thanks, A. Buyer

Step 2 — firm (Oct 6)

Hi Sam, PO 4471 is now 5 days past its Oct 1 ship date and I still don't have a confirmed date. We need 2,000 brackets on our line by Oct 10. Please reply by noon tomorrow, Oct 7, with: a committed ship date per lot, the recovery plan, and whether you can ship 800 pieces by Oct 8 to cover the first week. If expedited freight is needed to meet Oct 10, please confirm in writing that it is at your cost, per our terms. A. Buyer

Step 3 — escalate (Oct 8)

Hi Jordan, I'm escalating PO 4471 (2,000 brackets, due to ship Oct 1). Despite three requests to Sam (copied), we have no committed ship date, and our line needs these parts on Oct 10. Could you call me today or reply by 5 p.m. with a committed date per lot and the recovery plan? To protect the line, we are also checking alternate supply in parallel, and I'd rather this order recover. I've copied my manager. A. Buyer

Each step adds one new thing: a reply-by, then the need date and a partial ask, then the manager. "At your cost, per our terms" belongs in Step 2 only if your PO terms actually say so; otherwise ask who pays. "Checking alternate supply in parallel" is a fact, not a threat—say it only if you're really doing it. Copy your own manager at Step 3, not before.

What makes a supplier email "strong" without being rude?

  • Specific facts: PO, part, quantity, dates
  • One reply-by time
  • Numbered asks
  • Your need date and its impact
  • Only consequences you're authorized to act on—no threats you can't keep

Write the right step with AI

You are a purchasing assistant. Here are the facts for a late purchase order: PO number, part, quantity, confirmed ship date, our need date, today's date, what we've already sent and when, and the supplier's replies so far [PASTE]. Our PO terms on expedite freight: [PASTE or 'unknown']. Decide which step of this ladder applies: 0 pre-ship check, 1 missed date, 2 firm, 3 escalate to the supplier's manager. Explain why in one line. Then draft that email in under 100 words: state the facts, give numbered asks and one reply-by time, and include our need date from step 2 on. No threats, no penalties and no promises that aren't in the terms I gave. If our terms are unknown, ask who pays for expedites rather than stating it.

What to check: confirm the PO facts in your ERP, not just the thread; check expedite language against the actual PO terms; make the reply-by realistic for the supplier's time zone; don't paste pricing or another customer's information (see the data-safety checklist); send escalations yourself—don't let a tool auto-send them.

After the parts arrive

Log the miss against on-time delivery on your supplier scorecard. Late suppliers also drive safety stock; see ChatGPT prompts for inventory management. If the recovery changed terms, send a short recap so both sides have the same record.

FAQ

How do you write a strong email to a supplier for a delay in delivery?

Lead with the PO, part, quantity and due date. Make numbered asks, give one reply-by time, state your need date from the firm step on, and only mention consequences you are authorized to act on.

Is there a sample email to a supplier for late delivery?

Yes. The Step 1 email on this page is a short, polite sample for the day after a missed ship date. Steps 0, 2 and 3 show how the wording changes as you escalate.

How do you ask a supplier to expedite an order?

Ask for a committed ship date per lot, whether a partial can go sooner, and who pays for expedited freight under your PO terms. Put the expedite ask in writing; don't assume the supplier will absorb it.

How often should you follow up on a purchase order?

A common pattern is a pre-ship check a few days before the confirmed date, a polite note the day after a miss, a firmer note a few days later, then an escalation to the supplier's manager as your need date approaches. Adjust for how critical the part is.

What do you do if a supplier doesn't respond?

Escalate to their manager with your need date, and start the backup source in parallel. Copy your own manager at that step so they are not surprised.

Can AI send PO follow-up emails automatically?

Some tools can draft them. Microsoft's Dynamics 365 Procurement Agent (production-ready preview) finds unconfirmed or late POs and "automatically generates a draft email" for review, per Microsoft Learn. Automatic sending without review needs a separate configuration. Review escalations yourself before they go out.
