Excess and obsolete inventory: a worked action plan for six SKUs, plus an AI prompt

Short answerFlag each SKU with a fixed rule, for example more than 12 months of supply or no movement in 180 days. Then pick one action per SKU: stop reorders, cancel open POs, transfer, return to vendor, rework, sell at a discount or scrap. Estimate the recovery as a range, and send any write-down to Finance for approval.

Fictional exampleExample Valve Co., its SKUs and every number below are made up. Recovery percentages are assumptions, shown as assumptions. Don't paste real cost files or export-controlled part data into an AI tool; see the data-safety checklist.

The quick excess-and-obsolete prompt is Prompt 4 in the inventory guide. This page adds what that prompt doesn't: a filled-in SKU table, a decision rule per action, a recovery range per SKU, and the write-down hand-off to Finance.

What's the difference between excess and obsolete inventory?

ExcessObsolete
MeaningStill usable, but more than you'll use in a reasonable horizonNo future demand (superseded, design change, or no movement)
Typical flagMonths of supply above your thresholdNo movement for N days, or a supersession note
First questionCan we stop buying and use it down?Can we return, rework, sell or must we write it down?

What rule should you use to flag E&O?

Months of supply = on hand ÷ average monthly usage (use forward demand if you have it). This page uses the same example thresholds as our prompt kit: more than 12 months of supply, or no movement in 180 days. Agree your own thresholds with Finance before the first review.

Which action fits which SKU?

Work the questions in this order (based on the VMA / Swain Smith inventory-disposal procedure's order of options: return to supplier, ask about a restocking fee, transfer, sell to a third party, scrap; Accounting processes write-offs—VMA M2-03-14):

  1. Open PO → cancel or push out first
  2. Another site uses it → transfer
  3. Supplier will take it back → return to vendor (ask about the restocking fee)
  4. Superseded but reworkable → rework (engineering approval)
  5. Usable by others → discount sale or broker
  6. No value → scrap or donate (Finance and EHS rules)

What to hand Finance

Give them the write-down exposure range and the evidence behind each row. Under FASB ASU 2015-11, inventory measured by methods other than LIFO and the retail inventory method is measured at the lower of cost and net realizable value—"the estimated selling prices in the ordinary course of business, less reasonably predictable costs of completion, disposal, and transportation" (ASU 2015-11). Most companies use that rule; your controller decides the reserve. This page is not accounting advice.

Do it with AI

You are an inventory analyst. From the export below (SKU, on hand, unit cost, average monthly usage, last movement date, open PO qty, supersession note, other-site usage), flag every SKU with more than [12] months of supply or no movement in [180] days. For each flagged SKU, pick one action (cancel/push out PO, transfer, return to vendor, rework, discount sale, scrap, hold) and give the reason in one line. Estimate recovery as a low–high range and state the % assumption you used. Never recommend scrapping a SKU with open demand or open orders. Return a table, then totals: value flagged, recovery range, write-down exposure range, and open-PO value to cancel. List every SKU with missing data instead of guessing.

What to check: confirm usage with forward demand; call the supplier before counting RTV money; get engineering sign-off on rework; let Finance own the reserve; recompute one row by hand.

How to stop it coming back

Three common drivers: MOQs that force overbuys, forecast bias (see ChatGPT for demand planning), and engineering changes without a disposition plan. A Crowe working-capital case study listed four related causes of excess: inadequate forecasting and SIOP, poorly managed stocking strategies, insufficient management of procurement parameters, and no product life-cycle guidelines (Crowe). FAQ

What's the difference between excess and obsolete inventory?

Excess is still usable but more than you'll need. Obsolete has no future demand—superseded, redesigned or with no movement.

How do you calculate excess inventory?

Months of supply = on hand ÷ average monthly usage (or forward demand). Flag SKUs above your threshold, such as 12 months.

How do you treat obsolete inventory?

Work an action order: cancel open POs, transfer, return to vendor, rework, discount sale, then scrap. Get the right approvals for rework and write-downs.

What is an E&O reserve, and who sets it?

For most cost methods, inventory is measured at the lower of cost and net realizable value (FASB ASU 2015-11). Your controller sets the reserve and any write-off. This page does not give journal entries or tax advice.

How does return to vendor work?

Ask the supplier for an RMA, ask about restocking fees, and confirm the credit before you ship anything back.

How do you prevent obsolete inventory?

Tighten MOQs, watch forecast bias, and require a disposition plan with every engineering change.

Can ChatGPT find slow-moving inventory?

It can flag SKUs from an anonymized export against your rule and suggest actions with recovery ranges. Recheck the math and never let it invent demand.
