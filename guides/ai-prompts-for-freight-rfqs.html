AI prompts for freight RFQs, with a worked LTL quote comparison

Short answerCompare freight quotes on all-in cost per shipment, not the base rate: base linehaul, plus the fuel surcharge (a percentage that moves with the weekly diesel price), plus the accessorials your shipments will actually trigger, such as liftgate or appointments. Weight accessorials by how often they occur, then check transit time and claims terms before awarding.

A freight RFQ is mostly structured writing and comparison work, which AI assistants handle well. The prompts below work in any general AI assistant. Replace the [BRACKETS] with your own details, and anonymize your data first: no confidential rates, customer names, or export-controlled information. Follow your company's AI policy. See the data-safety checklist.

1. Clean up your lane data

Here is 12 months of shipment history (origin zip3, destination zip3, mode, weight, pallets, ship date): [PASTE]. Group it into lanes, calculate annual volume, average and peak weekly loads, and seasonality per lane, and flag records that look like errors. Output a table I can attach to an RFQ.

2. Draft the RFQ document

Draft a freight RFQ for [MODE: FTL/LTL/ocean/air] covering the attached lanes. Include: scope and term, volume disclaimer, service requirements (transit, tender acceptance, tracking), accessorial schedule to be quoted, fuel surcharge method, insurance, payment terms, bid format, deadline and evaluation criteria. Use [PLACEHOLDERS] for anything company-specific.

3. Write clarification questions

Act as an experienced carrier sales rep reading this RFQ: [PASTE]. List every ambiguity that would make you pad your price, and suggest wording that removes each one.

4. Normalize and compare bids

Here are bids from Carrier A/B/C for the same lanes: [PASTE]. Convert each to all-in cost per load and per unit using our expected volumes, including fuel at [FUEL INDEX VALUE] and likely accessorials. Show a comparison table per lane, the cheapest compliant bid, and the cost of splitting awards between primary and backup carriers.

5. Catch accessorial and terms traps

Review these carrier terms and accessorial schedules: [PASTE]. Flag detention or free-time terms, liftgate and residential fees, reweigh or reclass rules, minimums, and anything that differs from our RFQ. Estimate the annual cost of each at our volumes.

6. Award scenarios

Build 3 award scenarios (lowest cost, balanced risk, incumbent-friendly) from this comparison: [PASTE]. For each one, show total annual cost, number of carriers, and risk if the primary carrier rejects tenders. Recommend one and explain the trade-off in 100 words.

7. Award and regret emails

Write a short, professional award email to a winning carrier confirming lanes, rates, effective date and next steps, plus a courteous regret email to unsuccessful bidders that leaves the door open for future bids.

What should a freight RFQ include?

  • Lanes (origin and destination ZIP)
  • Annual shipments per lane
  • Weight, dimensions, pallet count and density
  • NMFC class basis (confirm under the current scale; see the NMFTA note above)
  • Required accessorials and how often they occur
  • Fuel surcharge basis (which table and which index)
  • Transit time requirement
  • Liability and claims terms
  • Quote validity

Use prompt 2 to draft the document from that checklist. Comparing supplier part quotes instead (Incoterms, currency, MOQ)? See the RFQ bid comparison example.

Good practice

  • Check every calculation the AI produces; ask it to "show the math".
  • Keep a human decision on awards and on any contract terms; have legal review contracts.
  • Use Incoterms and customs details only after checking the current official rules or with a licensed broker.

FAQ

What is an RFQ in logistics?

An RFQ (request for quotation) in logistics asks carriers or forwarders to price a defined set of lanes and services. You send the volumes, shipment profile and terms; they send rates you can compare.

What should a freight RFQ include?

Lanes, annual shipments, weight and dimensions, density and NMFC class basis, required accessorials and their frequency, the fuel surcharge basis, transit time, liability and claims terms, and quote validity. Prompt 2 on this page drafts that document.

What's the difference between a freight RFQ and an RFP?

An RFQ asks for prices against a defined scope. An RFP (request for proposal) usually invites a broader solution: service design, technology, coverage and price. Many freight "RFP" processes are really RFQs with a few service questions attached.

What are LTL accessorial charges?

Accessorials are fees for services beyond the base linehaul, such as liftgate, appointments, residential delivery, detention, reweigh or redelivery. Some are only triggered once the driver arrives, so they can appear on the invoice after the shipment. ODFL's accessorial explainer covers the common ones.

How is the fuel surcharge calculated?

Carriers publish their own fuel tables, often keyed to the U.S. EIA weekly diesel price. EIA says it publishes the price data many companies use, but "Every company has its own method for calculating fuel surcharges." Compare every bid with the same week's index.

Why did my LTL invoice come in higher than the quote?

Common causes are a reweigh or reclass after pickup, and accessorials that were not on the quote (detention, liftgate, redelivery). Check the invoice against the quote and the BOL, then use a freight-audit process.
