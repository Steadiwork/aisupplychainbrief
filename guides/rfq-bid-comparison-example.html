RFQ bid comparison example: a filled-in worksheet

Short answerTo compare RFQ bids, put every quote in one table and convert each to a total landed cost per unit: unit price, freight, one-time tooling spread over volume, and the carrying cost of any MOQ above your need. Score the non-price factors with weights agreed in advance, and turn every exception into a written question before you award. The fictional example below shows each step.

Fictional exampleEvery company, supplier, price and date on this page is made up for illustration. Don't paste real supplier quotes, pricing under NDA or any export-controlled data into an AI tool; anonymize first and follow your company's AI policy.

Comparing quotes is where an RFQ is won or lost. The sticker price is rarely the whole story: tooling, freight, minimum order quantities and the "exceptions" a supplier quietly adds can flip the result. Below is a complete, filled-in comparison for a simple buy, so you can see the layout, the math and the questions before you build your own.

The scenario

Example Outdoor Gear Co. (fictional) ships online orders in a printed corrugated carton. The buyer sent an RFQ to three suppliers for:

  • Item: regular slotted carton, 18 × 12 × 10 in, 32 ECT, 2-color print
  • Annual volume: 120,000 cartons (about 10,000 a month), forecast, not a commitment
  • Required lead time: 10 business days or less
  • Requested terms: Net 45, delivered price or freight quoted separately, price held 12 months

Step 2: Normalize to a year-one landed cost

Put every quote on the same basis: (unit price + freight) × annual volume + one-time tooling. Then add the cost of carrying the stock each MOQ forces you to hold. This example uses an assumed 25% annual carrying rate on average cycle stock (MOQ ÷ 2); use your own finance number.

Landed unit cost$0.88$0.78$0.89
Annual parts + freight (× 120,000)$105,600$93,600$106,800
+ Tooling$450$1,200$0
+ Carrying cost of cycle stock$550 (2,500 × $0.88 × 25%)$975 (5,000 × $0.78 × 25%)$278 (1,250 × $0.89 × 25%)
Year-one total$106,600$95,775$107,078

On cost alone, Supplier B is about $10,800 a year below A. But B misses the 10-day lead time and wants a 12-month commitment the RFQ didn't offer, and C didn't quote the spec at all.

Step 3: Score what the price doesn't show

Weights add up to 100 and were set before the bids came in. Scores run 1 (poor) to 5 (best).

A and B tie. That's useful: the decision now turns on two answers you can get with one email each, not on a gut call.

Step 4: Gaps and clarification questions

  • Supplier A: Which index, how often, and is there a cap? Will you hold the price for 6 months before the first adjustment? Can you match Net 45?
  • Supplier B: What is the price without the 12-month blanket order? Can you meet a 10-business-day lead time if we share a rolling forecast? When can we approve a sample?
  • Supplier C: Please quote the 2-color print in the RFQ. Does the $0.85 or the tooling waiver change?

Step 5: The one-paragraph recommendation

Recommendation (draft): Hold the award until two answers are in. Supplier B is lowest on year-one cost ($95,775 vs. $106,600 for A) but needs a 12-month commitment and misses our 10-day lead time. If B drops the commitment and meets 10 days, award to B. If not, award to A, and negotiate a 6-month price hold before index adjustments. Supplier C is out unless it quotes the 2-color spec. Assumptions: 120,000 cartons a year, 25% carrying rate.

Do it with AI in 5 minutes

Paste anonymized quotes (Supplier A, B, C) into your approved AI assistant with this prompt:

You are a senior buyer. Below are our RFQ requirements and three anonymized supplier quotes.
Requirements: [ITEM, ANNUAL VOLUME, REQUIRED LEAD TIME, REQUESTED TERMS]
Quotes (one row per supplier: unit price, freight, one-time tooling, lead time, MOQ, payment terms, exceptions taken): [PASTE TABLE]
Carrying-cost rate: [X% per year]
1) Build a year-one landed cost per supplier: (unit price + freight) x annual volume + tooling + carrying cost of average cycle stock (MOQ / 2 x landed unit cost x carrying rate). Show your math.
2) Score each supplier 1-5 on cost, lead time vs. requirement, meets spec, and terms, using these weights: [WEIGHTS ADDING TO 100]. Show the weighted total.
3) List every gap or exception per supplier and write one clarification question for each.
4) Draft a 4-sentence recommendation that names what would change the decision.
List any missing or inconsistent data instead of guessing. Use tables for steps 1 and 2. Keep the answer under 350 words.

FAQ

How do you compare RFQ bids fairly?

Normalize them first. Put every quote in the same table and convert each to a total landed cost per unit, including freight, one-time costs spread over volume and any extra inventory caused by minimum order quantities. Then score non-price factors such as quality, delivery and risk with weights you set before opening the bids.

What is landed cost in a bid comparison?

Landed cost is the full cost to get the item to your door. It's the unit price plus freight, plus one-time costs like tooling spread over the expected volume, plus costs such as carrying extra inventory when a supplier's minimum order is above what you need. It's the number to compare, not the sticker price.

How should I weight price against quality and delivery?

Set the weights before you see the quotes, and write down what a low, medium and high score means for each factor so scoring is consistent. The right weights depend on the item. The worked example on this page uses one fictional set of weights, not a recommended standard.

What should I do with supplier exceptions?

List every exception or assumption a supplier added, such as price validity, payment terms or index-linked pricing, and send each one back as a written question before you award. An exception you didn't price in can change which bid is actually lowest.

Can AI compare RFQ bids for me?

It can build the comparison table, calculate landed cost with the formulas shown and list gaps and exceptions as questions. Anonymize the quotes first, use your company's approved tool, recheck the math in a spreadsheet, and make the award decision yourself.
