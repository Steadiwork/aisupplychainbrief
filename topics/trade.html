Mexico's customs agency shows a tool that flags undervalued imports

Undervaluation is the target. Mexico's National Customs Agency (ANAM) demonstrated its Value Intelligence Platform, built to flag imports that may be declared below their real value, at the CONECTAA 2026 customs brokers' meeting, reported October 23. Sessions there, including a workshop with Google, covered AI for tariff classification, catching errors in customs declarations and predicting risk. Mexico Business News notes that automated flags don't prove wrongdoing on their own; people still review them.

Treasury proposes new rules on US investment in foreign AI and chip companies

Comments are due December 7. The US Treasury proposed rules on October 22 under a 2025 law, the COINS Act, that would make US persons notify Treasury of certain investments in companies from countries of concern working on semiconductors, AI, quantum, supercomputing or hypersonics, and ban others outright. Treasury also proposed adding biotechnology. AP reports the targets include China, Russia and North Korea. The rules are a draft and can change before they're final.

Read the story →

So whatWeighing a JV or stake in a chip or AI supplier abroad? Flag it to legal; comments are open until Dec. 7.

China drafts export controls on AI and solar manufacturing technology

40 technologies, 23 of them in AI. On October 16, China's commerce and science ministries published a draft adding large models, AI agents and computing clusters, plus 12 solar manufacturing technologies covering polysilicon, wafers, cells and the key equipment that carries them, to the country's restricted-export technology list. Comments run until November 15, but registration starts now: transferring or licensing listed technology abroad, or shifting its ownership or control to a foreign entity, must be registered first. Technology already open-sourced or published is excluded.

So whatIf you license or move AI or solar know-how out of China, check whether it now needs pre-export registration.

Taiwan plans to make unauthorized AI chip shipments to China a crime

Taiwan's economics minister, Kung Ming-hsin, told Bloomberg that Taipei will add AI servers and leading-edge semiconductors to its list of controlled goods, in line with commitments under its trade agreement with the US. Once the change takes effect, shipping restricted AI hardware to China without authorization would become a criminal offense under Taiwan's Foreign Trade Act. Today, Taiwan can only pursue suspected smugglers under other laws. No start date has been given.

So whatExpect tighter screening on AI servers and accelerators that ship from or through Taiwan.

Chip tariffs: the data-center exemption is still in limbo

January's Section 232 tariff put 25% on certain advanced logic chips but exempted chips headed for US data centers, pending a Commerce Department review that hasn't been published. On October 2, five tech trade groups including CCIA and TechNet asked the White House to keep any further chip, robotics and machinery tariffs targeted, and to extend any tariff offset program to downstream buyers. No second-phase decision has been announced.
