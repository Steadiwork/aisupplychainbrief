SAP starts sending warehouse tasks straight to robots, and logging what they did

A humanoid robot at Arçelik-LG now takes its inbound storage tasks from SAP and records the result there. SAP showed the setup at TechEd on October 27 as part of new physical AI capabilities that send tasks from SAP Extended Warehouse Management to robots and write status and exceptions back. In a proof of concept at Intel's Chengdu site, SAP and Intel say material moves were automated end to end, with shorter transport distances and less manual handling; neither company gave figures.

GXO takes Digit humanoid robots to two more U.S. warehouses

More than 100,000 totes. That's how many Agility Robotics' Digit humanoid has moved at GXO's Flowery Branch, Georgia, warehouse under a robots-as-a-service deal signed in 2024, GXO said on October 26. GXO now plans to put Digit to work on repetitive tote handling at e-commerce sites in Statesville, North Carolina, and Lebanon, Tennessee, starting in early 2027. The companies say Digit has logged more than 65,000 hours of real-world operation across all of its deployments.

Dexory's scanning robots now flag open boxes and misaligned pallets

Open boxes, goods pulled forward and misaligned pallets are now on the list of rack problems Dexory's autonomous scanning robots can flag, the company announced October 21. Its Storage Health feature added NVIDIA's Cosmos-Reason2-2B model, fine-tuned on Dexory's own warehouse images, as a second check when its existing models aren't confident. Dexory says it tested the 2B and 8B versions and picked the smaller one for accuracy and speed. Robotics 24/7 covered the update October 23.

Robots sorting Zalando returns now get tested fixes in days, not weeks

Days instead of weeks. That's how fast Sereact, NVIDIA, Nebius, CEVA Logistics and Zalando say a robot mistake on the returns line can now come back as a tested fix, under a training setup they announced on October 21. Recordings from the returns stations are turned into thousands of simulated variations, used to retrain Sereact's Cortex robot software, and scored against a pass mark before any update goes back to the floor. The first use is Zalando returns at CEVA sites, where Sereact's dual-arm stations went live in Greven, Germany and Świebodzin, Poland on October 7. The speed figure is the companies' own.

Robots are learning to pick shoeboxes at an s.Oliver warehouse

Shoeboxes have been close to impossible for general-purpose picking robots, according to Nomagic, and s.Oliver Group now plans to put the startup's Shoebox Picker to work. The fashion group selected Nomagic on October 14 to automate induction of a wide range of items, shoeboxes included, at its logistics site in Dettelbach, Germany, using AI vision and flexible robot handling. Neither company has given a go-live date, robot count or pick rates.

Robots are sorting Zalando's fashion returns without item-by-item training

Sereact's two-armed robots are now sorting Zalando returns in live operations at CEVA Logistics sites in Greven, Germany, and Świebodzin, Poland. Sereact says its Cortex software lets the robots pick, identify and sort crumpled items they've never seen, without per-product setup or a fixed item list. The system is sold as robotics-as-a-service, and staff move to supervising stations, handling exceptions and checking quality. The companies call it the first step toward automating returns across Europe.

Ultra raises $50M to lease packing robots to warehouses by the month

Brooklyn-based Ultra closed a $50M Series A led by Framework Ventures, bringing total funding to $62M. It installs its OP1 packing robots at warehouses and third-party logistics sites for an upfront integration fee plus a monthly service fee, rather than a big capital purchase. The company says its robots have packed over 500,000 orders, and it relies on Physical Intelligence for the AI models that run them.
