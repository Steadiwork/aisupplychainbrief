Fragment launches with $56 million to chase purchasing and payment exceptions

The eighteen-cent invoice mismatch now has investors. Fragment launched on October 28 with $56 million in total funding, including a Series A led by 8VC and a seed round co-led by General Catalyst and A*. SK hynix says its DRAM and HBM output is effectively sold out through 2029

Sold out through 2029. SK hynix said on its third-quarter earnings call on October 28 that, counting long-term agreements and deals under discussion, its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory output for the next three years is effectively spoken for, and it expects memory demand to outrun supply through 2030. The company said customer requests for HBM are more than twice what it can supply, and that long-term agreements already cover more than half its capacity for 2027 and 2028. It also expects PC and phone shipments to fall as memory costs rise.

So whatAsk distributors now for 2027 memory allocation and pricing in writing.

TSMC weighs up to 20% higher prices for AI chip packaging

Six to eight months. That's how long equipment lead times for advanced chip packaging have stretched, up from three to six, as TSMC weighs raising prices for its CoWoS packaging by 10% to 20%, Taiwan's Commercial Times reported, citing supply chain sources. CoWoS joins AI processors to high-bandwidth memory in data center accelerators, and demand has spread from GPUs to server CPUs and custom chips. TSMC has not confirmed the plan. TrendForce, citing the Liberty Times, says TSMC has also set wafer price increases of 3% to 6% starting January 2027.

AI data center tenants now ask $5 million a day when builds run late

$5 million a day, up from $1 million this spring. That's what tenants in new AI data center leases are asking developers to pay when power and buildings aren't ready on time, The Information reported on October 25. Insurance broker Marsh designed a delay-in-delivery policy this spring for the $1 million case, then saw new deals ask for five times that within months. Marsh says contractors and suppliers can be asked to backstop those penalties in their own agreements, and that guarantees now cover equipment lead times and energization dates.

Micron's Taiwan union plans a Thursday strike at an AI memory hub

A strike set for Thursday, October 22, at Micron's plant in Taoyuan, Taiwan, where the company makes DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI servers. The Taoyuan union announced it on October 19 to back a second union in a bonus dispute, timing it to that union's mediation meeting with Micron the same day, Reuters reported. The union hasn't said how long the walkout will last, and TrendForce says any hit to output depends on its size, its length and Micron's contingency plans.

BMW's procurement agent answers capacity questions in minutes, and its language model does no math

Weeks down to a few minutes: that's how long BMW says it now takes to answer a question like "can we build more iX3s?" with its procurement agent, Mr. Flexibility, which it showed to journalists, automotiveIT reported on October 12. The agent pulls together the production program, agreed supplier capacity and engineering data, including battery-cell capacity at 13 supplier sites. The design is the lesson: the language model "calculates nothing," product owner Maik Leuthold told automotiveIT. Tested, rule-based tools do the math, each user sees only what they're allowed to see, stale data gets flagged, and people make the call. The weeks-to-minutes figure is BMW's own.

Read the story →

So whatLet AI pick the tool but keep the math in calculations you trust, and date-stamp your supplier capacity data.

Microsoft walks away from the UK's biggest planned AI data center over power

No grid power until the 2030s: that's what Nscale was told about its planned data center in Loughton, Essex, which was meant to house about 23,000 Nvidia chips and open next year. Microsoft, its only publicly announced customer for the site, has ended its agreement, the BBC and the Guardian reported on October 18; Microsoft declined to comment. Nscale says it remains committed, is exploring alternative power sources, and that its Microsoft projects in Norway, Portugal and the US are unaffected.
